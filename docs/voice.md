# Voice

`crates/voice` is the voice connection core, ported from oxidcord's `crates/voice` (MIT/Apache); video receive came with v1 103 ("Video" below) and your own camera with v1 104 ("Camera" below). It has no UI dependency. The session drives it (below, "Joining"); DAVE end-to-end encryption comes from `crates/dave` (below, "DAVE"). The transport decision (UDP) is in `docs/fingerprint.md`, "Voice".

## Shape

- `VoiceConnection::connect(VoiceParams, VoiceOptions)` runs the handshake and returns once the session description has arrived and media is running. `VoiceParams` holds the guild (or none for DMs, where `server_id` is the channel), the channel, the user, the main gateway's session id, the voice token and the endpoint from VOICE_SERVER_UPDATE. `VoiceOptions` holds the audio mode, the TLS `Trust`, and the user agent and locale for the upgrade. Every connection has its own `DaveSession`; its frame crypto sits in the media path behind the `FrameTransform` trait, and the supervisor feeds it the DAVE opcodes.
- `signal.rs`: the voice websocket on `net::ws`, the handshake (identify, ready, IP discovery, select protocol, session description, op 5, op 15) and resume.
- `supervisor.rs`: the running connection. It reads frames, forwards op 5 for the media task, and decides what a close means (below).
- `transport.rs`: the `Transport` trait (send a sealed RTP packet, receive and open one) and its only implementation, UDP with IP discovery. A WebRTC transport would implement the same trait.
- `media.rs`: the 10 ms media loop. Capture is resampled to 48 kHz mono, run through the high-pass filter and echo cancellation, noise suppression (DeepFilterNet3 by default) and automatic gain control (below, "Echo cancellation and gain control", "Noise suppression"), scaled by the input volume, gated (voice activity above the sensitivity threshold with a 15-frame hangover, or push to talk while the key is held), encoded with libopus (FEC, DTX; the bitrate follows the channel, below) and sent. Receive goes through a per-SSRC jitter buffer and Opus decoder into the mixer; a watched stream's mixed frame is added after the master volume (below, "Go Live"); every mixed frame is the echo canceller's playback reference, then goes to the device or the manual output. A Go Live connection that shares sound sends it on its own path (below, "Stream sound").
- `audio.rs`: cpal devices on their own thread. `AudioIo::Manual` skips devices: `feed_input` queues 20 ms frames and `take_output` returns every mixed frame with the SSRCs that made it (none for silence). Tests use only the manual path.
- `processing.rs`: `Processor`, WebRTC's audio processing through `sonora` (below, "Echo cancellation and gain control").
- `sound.rs`: stream sound, `SoundSource`, `SoundTarget`, the WASAPI process loopback (`sound/loopback.rs`), `ToneSource`, and `PlaybackFeed`, which carries a watched stream's mixed frames into the call (below, "Stream sound").
- `diagnostics.rs`: `MicCheck`, the mic test (below, "Settings").

## Echo cancellation and gain control

WebRTC's audio processing module, the native library under Discord's desktop media engine, through `sonora` 0.2.0, a pure-Rust port of WebRTC M145's APM (v1 116). `voice::Processor` (`processing.rs`) holds two instances, both 48 kHz mono at the full-band processing rate:

- Echo cancellation (`voice.echo_cancellation`, on by default): WebRTC's high-pass filter and AEC3. Every mixed 20 ms frame, after per-user volumes, the output volume and deafen and right before the device (or the manual output), is downmixed to mono and given to AEC3 in two 10 ms chunks as the playback reference (`process_render_f32`), silence included. Each microphone frame then goes through the high-pass filter and AEC3 with the delay set by `set_stream_delay_ms`: the playback still queued for the device plus cpal's input and output buffer latency, read from the callback timestamps (`Rings::echo_delay`; 0 with `AudioIo::Manual`). AEC3 also finds the delay itself. WebRTC's own noise suppressor stays off.
- Automatic gain control (`voice.auto_gain_control`, on by default): AGC2's adaptive digital controller (RNN voice activity detection, 5 dB headroom, at most 50 dB of gain changing by at most 6 dB a second, output noise held under -50 dBFS) and its limiter, in the second instance so it runs after noise suppression. The OS microphone volume is never touched.
- The capture order is 48 kHz, high-pass and AEC3, noise suppression, AGC2, input volume, meter and gate, Opus. A switch applies to the next frame without reconnecting; turning one off drops its state and turning it on again starts fresh. The high-pass filter goes with echo cancellation.
- Only the call's connection runs it: a stream connection (`ConnectionKind::Stream`, watching or going live) ignores `VoiceConnection::set_processing`, and the sound you share when going live never goes through it. A stream you watch plays through the call's mixer (below, "Go Live"), so its sound is in the call's reference and is cancelled from your microphone like anyone in the call; sounds from other apps are not, as with any echo canceller inside one app. The mic test runs the same chain with its played-back voice as the reference.
- `VoiceStats::frames_echo_cancelled` and `frames_gain_controlled` count the frames each one processed.

Measured on this machine (`cargo run --release -p kyoko-voice --example processing_bench`, 20-thread desktop CPU, with other builds running), per 10 ms chunk over 1050 chunks (five passes over the 4.2 s speech fixture) played and echoed back at half level with 50 ms of delay over room noise:

| | mean | median | p99 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Playback reference (AEC3 render) | 39 us | 34 us | 141 us |
| High-pass and AEC3 | 178 us | 164 us | 464 us |
| AGC2 | 78 us | 67 us | 217 us |
| Whole chain | 295 us | 265 us | 833 us |

That is about 3% of one core next to DeepFilterNet3's 1.28 ms, well inside the 10 ms budget, so it runs inline. It is far from `sonora`'s own figure (13.3 us for AEC3, its noise suppressor and AGC2 at 48 kHz on an Apple M4 Max built with `-C target-cpu=native`, which the research note put at about 20 us on a 5950X): here AEC3 alone is about 150 us. Capping the internal rate at 32 kHz, `sonora`'s default, only brings the whole chain to 236 us, so kyoko keeps the full 48 kHz band. The release exe grows by about 1.0 MB (118.4 MB at v1 116, which also carries v1 117, against 117.4 MB with v1 115; `cargo build --release -p kyoko`).

Through a call (the session tests, below), with the mixer's output fed back into the microphone 65 ms later at half level, the echo that reaches mockcord drops by a median of about 65 dB per second once AEC3 has converged (about 3 seconds; DTX then often sends nothing) and by 0.3 dB with echo cancellation off; your own voice over the far end loses about 2 dB. Gain control takes speech at -45 dBFS to about -25 dBFS (the gain stops near 20 dB because the fixture's own hiss would pass the -50 dBFS noise limit) and leaves speech at -15 dBFS at about -16 dBFS; with it off, -45 dBFS goes out at -46 dBFS.

`sonora` was checked before it was taken (2026-10-29): BSD-3-Clause, 0.2.0 from 2026-07-29, pinned with `=0.2.0`; about 720 Rust tests, many ported from WebRTC's own unit tests, run in CI on Linux, macOS, Windows, Android and iOS, with a weekly fuzz run; comparison tests against the C++ APM per component (filter bank and high-pass to 5e-5) and for the whole pipeline; and the WebRTC C++ test suite (2389 tests, 2368 passed and 21 skipped in the run of 2026-10-26) built against the Rust pipeline through `sonora-ffi` on Linux and macOS. Most of that suite tests the C++ components themselves; only the APM-level tests go through the Rust code, and the bit-exact `ApmTest.Process` is disabled for it (its reference was made with AGC1). The crate has one maintainer and describes itself as an AI-assisted port. Later fixes on its main branch (an AEC3 render buffer headroom fix for underruns, among others) are not released yet.

## Noise suppression

`crates/denoise` (`kyoko-denoise`, headless) removes background noise from the microphone on your side, as Krisp does in Discord (v1 115). Removing other people's voices (Krisp's voice cancellation) is out of scope. The setting `voice.noise_suppression` (`store::NoiseSuppression`) picks one of:

- **High Quality** (`high_quality`, the default): DeepFilterNet3 (`src/deep_filter/`, a port of the inference path of DeepFilterNet's `libDF` to tract 0.23, the model read as NNEF; full-band 48 kHz, 960-sample FFT, 480-sample hop, the standard model with 2 frames of lookahead). Each 20 ms capture frame is two 10 ms hops, run inline in the media loop. The model (`models/DeepFilterNet3.nnef.tar.gz`, embedded in the exe) is hash-checked and turned into tract plans once per process on a `denoise-load` thread the first time a connection or mic test asks for it (`denoise::prepare`), about 0.2 s in a release build; until it is ready, and if it ever fails to load, the frames go through RNNoise instead, so suppression is on from the first frame.
- **Low CPU** (`low_cpu`): RNNoise from xiph's 0.2 line (`csrc/rnnoise/`, the 2025-01 model, built with `cc`; its SSE4.1 and AVX2 kernels are picked at run time), on 480-sample hops. Its weights (`models/rnnoise.bin`, 3.5 MB of int8 and float arrays in RNNoise's blob format) are embedded and hash-checked.
- **Off**: the microphone passes as it is.

Noise suppression runs right after echo cancellation and before automatic gain control, the input volume, the meter and the gate, in calls and in the mic test. `VoiceStats::frames_denoised` counts the frames a suppressor processed and `VoiceStats::suppressor` names the one that ran last (`voice::Suppression`), so a test can wait for DeepFilterNet3 to take over. Switching drops the other suppressor's state; the new one starts fresh.

Measured on this machine (a release build of `cargo run --release -p kyoko-denoise --features testing --example bench`, 20-thread desktop CPU, with two other builds running), per 10 ms hop over 990 hops of speech in noise:

| | mean | median | p99 | added latency |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| High Quality (DeepFilterNet3) | 1.28 ms | 1.21 ms | 2.40 ms | 30 ms (10 ms STFT overlap and 20 ms lookahead) |
| Low CPU (RNNoise) | 0.24 ms | 0.21 ms | 0.42 ms | 10 ms |

Both stay well under the 10 ms budget, so they run inline rather than on a thread of their own. On the bench clip (4.2 s of a public-domain LibriVox reading, `crates/denoise/fixtures/README.md`, with generated pink noise and mains hum) High Quality raises SI-SDR by 8.1, 6.6 and 1.1 dB at 0, 5 and 10 dB SNR and RNNoise by 6.2, 4.6 and 2.6 dB (High Quality gains least at 10 dB, likely because it also removes the 2006 recording's own background hiss, which SI-SDR counts as part of the reference); High Quality takes noise-only stretches down by 31 to 46 dB and RNNoise by 51 to 55 dB, and both keep the speech level within 1.1 dB. Through a call (the session tests, below) Opus caps SI-SDR: clean speech comes back at about 7 dB, since Opus in VoIP mode does not keep the waveform. There, against Off at the same SNR, High Quality gains 5.2, 3.2 and 1.1 dB at 0, 5 and 10 dB and Low CPU 3.7, 1.8 and -0.1 dB; noise-only stretches drop by more than 40 dB with either (DTX then often sends nothing), and speech stays within 1.4 dB.

DeepFilterNet3 normalises its features with running means over about a second. After a pure tone, which leaves every other band at digital silence, it takes more than 1.5 s to start suppressing noise again (the bench's "after tone" line); a real microphone always has a noise floor, so the tests warm a connection up on generated room noise, not on the test tone. RNNoise is not affected.

The release exe grows by 28.4 MB (89.0 MB on main at dd4f323, 117.4 MB with v1 115, `cargo build --release -p kyoko`): 11.5 MB of it is the two embedded weight files (DeepFilterNet3 8.0 MB, RNNoise 3.5 MB) and the rest is `tract-core`, `tract-linalg`'s kernels and `tract-nnef`. Reading the model as ONNX instead took 8.0 MB more (`tract-onnx` with its protobuf reader, and `tract-pulse` with the `tract-transformers` it pulls in), so the model ships converted to NNEF: `cargo run --release -p kyoko-denoise --features onnx,testing --example nnef_export -- DeepFilterNet3_onnx.tar.gz models/DeepFilterNet3.nnef.tar.gz` rebuilds it from the upstream ONNX archive (`https://github.com/Rikorose/DeepFilterNet/raw/d375b2d8309e0935d165700c91da9de862a99c31/models/DeepFilterNet3_onnx.tar.gz`, SHA-256 checked by the tool), pulsed and decluttered as the ONNX path did, and checks that both produce the same samples; on the three bench clips they are bit-identical. The written NNEF tars carry their write time, so a rebuilt file has a new hash and `DEEP_FILTER_SHA256` in `src/lib.rs` changes with it.

## Wire

Facts from docs.discord.food voice connections and close codes (userdoccers master, read 2026-10-28):

- `v=9`. Identify keys in the docs' order: `server_id`, `channel_id`, `user_id`, `session_id`, `token`, `video: true`, `streams` (two video layers, `rid` 100 and 50), `max_dave_protocol_version: 1`. Resume: `server_id`, `channel_id`, `session_id`, `token`, `seq_ack`.
- Heartbeat every `min(heartbeat_interval, 5000)` ms, as the web client does, first beat one period after Hello, `{"t": unix ms, "seq_ack": last seq or -1}`. A server op 3 is answered at once. A missed ack counts as a lost connection.
- Select protocol: `protocol: "udp"`, `data` (`address`, `port`, `mode`), `codecs` (opus 120, H264 101/102), a fresh UUIDv4 `rtc_connection_id`, and Ready's `experiments` echoed back. AES-GCM rtpsize is chosen when offered, XChaCha20-Poly1305 rtpsize otherwise.
- One op 5 (`speaking: 0`) goes out right after the session description, before any media. Speech sends op 5 with flag 1 (VOICE); the end of speech sends five Opus silence frames (`f8 ff fe`) and then op 5 with 0.
- Upgrade headers are the gateway's: User-Agent, `Origin: https://discord.com`, Accept-Language, through `net::ws` with `UPGRADE_ORDER`. Each connection has its own wreq client with no cookie store, so no Cookie is sent.
- Never logged: the voice token, the secret key, raw payloads.

## DAVE

`crates/dave` (`kyoko-dave`, headless) is oxidcord's `dave/*` port: the MLS session, the sender key ratchet, the frame cryptor and the frame format, for DAVE protocol version 1 with MLS ciphersuite `MLS_128_DHKEMP256_AES128GCM_SHA256_P256`. Facts from docs.discord.food voice connections ("DAVE") and the DAVE whitepaper (daveprotocol.com), read 2026-10-28.

- Identify sends `max_dave_protocol_version: 1`. The session description's `dave_protocol_version` starts the session: with 1 the client creates its MLS signing key and sends its key package (op 26, binary); with 0 it sends and accepts plain Opus.
- Binary frames from the server carry a 2-byte big-endian `seq` before the opcode (it counts towards `seq_ack` like the JSON `seq`); binary frames from the client are the opcode and the payload only.
- Op 25 (external sender) creates the pending group with the external-senders extension. Op 27 (proposals, append or revoke) queues only Add and Remove proposals from the external sender, for users the call has announced (op 11, op 5), and commits them (op 28: the commit, then the welcome when someone is added). Op 29 (announced commit) and op 30 (welcome) move the group to the next epoch; the welcome must be for this channel's group, with the same external sender and only announced users.
- After a commit or welcome, each member's 16-byte media key base is the MLS exporter secret `"Discord Secure Frames v0"` with the member's user id as little-endian u64 context. The keys install at once for receiving, and the client sends op 23 (transition ready). Op 22 (execute transition) switches sending to the new key; transition id 0 applies at once. Old receive keys keep working for 10 seconds after a transition.
- A commit or welcome that cannot be processed sends op 31 (invalid commit or welcome) with its transition id, resets the group and sends a fresh key package, so the server can remove and re-add the member.
- Op 21 with version 0 (downgrade) lets plain frames in and, once executed, sends plain Opus; op 24 (prepare epoch) with epoch 1 starts a fresh group (upgrade).
- Frames: AES-128-GCM with an 8-byte tag, a 32-bit nonce (top 8 bits are the key generation) in a 12-byte IV, the tag, the ULEB128 nonce, the supplemental size and the `0xFAFA` marker appended. Opus is encrypted whole. The Opus silence frame `f8 ff fe` passes unencrypted both ways.
- `VoiceEvent::Dave { epoch, version, members }` follows each applied transition and `VoiceEvent::DaveFailure(reason)` each failure; `VoiceConnection::dave()` gives the counters (encrypted, decrypted, rejected, passed silence frames) and whether sending is encrypted. The session logs the epoch and member count at debug level. Secret keys, exporter secrets and MLS state are never logged.
- The MLS provider is kyoko's own, on BoringSSL (`docs/fingerprint.md`, "Voice", "DAVE crypto provider").

## Closes

| Close | What happens |
|---|---|
| none (network loss), 4015, and codes below 4000 | resume with backoff 1, 2, 4, 8, 8 s; a resume closed with 4006/4009 falls through to a fresh connect |
| 4006, 4009 | a fresh identify (new SSRC, new UDP socket, media restarted), up to 3 attempts |
| every other 4xxx | stop: `VoiceEvent::Stopped(reason)`, where 4014 is `Disconnected`, 4017 `E2eeRequired`, 4022 `CallEnded`, 4021 `RateLimited`, 4004 `AuthenticationFailed`, 4011 `ServerNotFound`, others `Closed(code)` |

Joins and rejoins only ever come from the caller; the core never re-joins after a stop.

## Joining

`crates/session/src/task/voice_link.rs` (ported from oxidcord's `voice_link.rs`) joins, moves and leaves server voice channels. DM and group calls are V12.

- `Command::JoinVoice { guild_id, channel_id }` checks that the channel is a voice or stage channel of that server (anything else is rejected, so a voice row can never start a DM call), sends op 4 and waits. VOICE_STATE_UPDATE for the current user gives the gateway `session_id`, VOICE_SERVER_UPDATE the voice `token` and `endpoint`; once both are in, `VoiceConnection::connect` runs with the identity's user agent and locale. Joining another channel drops the connection and sends op 4 again; the new VOICE_SERVER_UPDATE brings a new token, so the move connects afresh. A VOICE_SERVER_UPDATE while connected (a server change) reconnects too.
- `Command::LeaveVoice` disconnects and sends op 4 with the server's `guild_id` and `channel_id: null`.
- Op 4 keys, in the docs' order: `guild_id`, `channel_id`, `self_mute`, `self_deaf`, `self_video` (`false`), `preferred_region`, `preferred_regions`, `flags`. The regions are READY's `geo_ordered_rtc_regions` (the first is the preferred one), so nothing is fetched from discord.media. `flags` is 2 (`ALLOW_VOICE_RECORDING`); `CLIPS_ENABLED` is a desktop app feature and stays off.
- No answer within 15 seconds (a full channel gets no events) ends the join: op 4 with `channel_id: null` and a toast ("Couldn't connect to voice. The channel may be full."). A failed connection ends it the same way.
- A fresh gateway session (READY after an invalid session or a new identify) drops the voice connection and sends op 4 again for the same channel; Discord then sends a new state and server update and voice reconnects. A resumed gateway session changes nothing.
- A voice stop (`VoiceEvent::Stopped`) ends the call with a toast naming the reason; 4014 (removed from the channel) does not send op 4 again, since Discord already moved the user out. VOICE_STATE_UPDATE with `channel_id: null` for the current user ends the call the same way.
- Joins only ever come from a click; the session never rejoins on its own except to re-announce a channel after the gateway lost its session.

Mute and deafen (`Command::SetSelfMute`, `SetSelfDeaf`) work in and out of a call; `State::voice_self` keeps them between calls. Deafen implies mute: op 4 then carries `self_mute: true, self_deaf: true`, and undeafening restores the earlier mute. Unmuting while deafened undeafens too, as in Discord. In a call each change resends op 4 and is applied at once: the connection's mute stops sending (the voice gate is reset, so only the end-of-speech silence frames follow), deafen silences the mixer and implies mute. Server mute and deafen arrive in VOICE_STATE_UPDATE (`mute`, `deaf`); they are kept in the guild's voice states (`State::own_voice_state`) and also stop sending or playback.

`state.voice()` is the call: channel, server, `VoiceStatus` (Connecting, Connected, Reconnecting) and the self flags. `Session::voice_connection()` hands out the running `VoiceConnection` (tests feed and read audio through it), and `Session::set_voice_setup` picks the audio mode (devices by default, `AudioIo::Manual` in tests), the TLS trust for the voice server and the join timeout.

## In the call

Who is in the call, who speaks and how loud each person plays (v1 101):

- Peers: op 11 (clients connect) and op 13 (client disconnect) become `VoiceEvent::UserJoined` and `UserLeft`; the session keeps them as `VoiceSession::peers` (`LocalEvent::VoicePeer`). Leaving clears the person's speaking too.
- Speaking (`talk.rs`): op 5 maps an SSRC to a user and, with a non-zero `speaking`, marks them speaking at once; op 5 with 0 or op 13 ends it. Every mixed frame that decodes from a known SSRC above -50 dBFS keeps its user speaking, and someone not heard for 400 ms (`HANGOVER`) stops: Discord sends op 5 only when someone starts, so silence frames, DTX or no packets end the ring. Your own speech raises `VoiceEvent::Speaking` for your user id when the gate opens and closes (with the op 5 kyoko sends). The session applies them as `LocalEvent::Speaking` into `VoiceSession::speaking`.
- User volume and local mute: `Command::SetUserVolume { user_id, volume }` (0 to 200 percent) and `Command::SetUserMuted { user_id, muted }` keep a `state::UserAudio` per user (`State::user_audio`; the default 100 and unmuted is not stored). The mixer scales that user's decoded audio by `UserAudio::gain` (0 when muted) before the master volume, through `VoiceConnection::set_user_volume`; the connection gets every saved entry when it comes up. The list is saved per account in kv `voice.user_audio` (a JSON list of `{ user_id, volume, muted }`, bad entries skipped one by one) and loaded at launch. Your own user has no entry.
- Opus bitrate: the encoder starts at 64 kbps and follows the channel's `bitrate` (`VoiceConnection::set_bitrate`, clamped to 6 to 510 kbps; `bitrate()` is what the encoder runs at). The session sets it when the connection comes up and on every CHANNEL_UPDATE for the channel you are in.

## Settings

Settings > Voice & Video (v1 100) changes `store::VoiceSettings`, and the session applies them, live while connected:

- The desktop saves a change through `settings.set` (`bridge::SettingKey::Voice*`: `voice.input_device`, `voice.output_device`, `voice.input_volume`, `voice.output_volume` (0 to 200 percent), `voice.push_to_talk`, `voice.push_to_talk_release_delay` (0 to 2000 ms), `voice.auto_sensitivity`, `voice.sensitivity_db` (-100 to 0), `voice.noise_suppression` (`high_quality`, `low_cpu` or `off`), `voice.echo_cancellation`, `voice.auto_gain_control`) and sends `Command::ApplyVoiceSettings`; the session reads the store and applies it. A connection that comes up gets every setting too, so they hold across restarts.
- A device change while connected reopens only that cpal stream (`VoiceConnection::set_input_device`, `set_output_device`; the capture chain restarts) and never reconnects. `audio_devices()` and `audio_restarts()` say which devices are in use and how often they were reopened; in `AudioIo::Manual` the choice is recorded and counted, with nothing opened.
- Input sensitivity: automatic is a fixed -50 dB threshold (oxidcord's; Discord's adaptive gate is not ported); otherwise the slider's value. The gate and the meter read the level after noise suppression and the input volume.
- Push to talk: `Command::SetPushToTalk(held)` opens the gate while held. A release closes it after `voice.push_to_talk_release_delay` (default 20 ms, Discord's `pttReleaseDelay`): the session starts a timer (`Internal::PushToTalkReleased`) and a press before it fires cancels it, so the mic stays open. A delay of 0, or a release while the gate is already closed, closes it at once. `VoiceConnection::transmitting()` reads the gate.
- The desktop watches `keybinds.push_to_talk` through low-level keyboard and mouse hooks (`platform::push_to_talk::TalkHook`, `docs/desktop.md`, "Push to talk"), not a registered hotkey, so the key still reaches the focused app, as in Discord. The hooks exist only while Push to Talk is the input mode and a binding is set; changing either setting removes them, releases the key, and installs them again if both still hold.
- Noise suppression is a choice like Discord's (High Quality, Low CPU, Off; above, "Noise suppression"); switching applies to the next frame without reconnecting. Echo Cancellation and Automatic Gain Control are switches, both on by default, applied the same way (above, "Echo cancellation and gain control").
- Mic test: `Command::StartMicTest` and `StopMicTest`. Outside a call it starts `voice::MicCheck` (oxidcord's `diagnostics` loopback): capture, the same processing as a call (echo cancellation with the played-back voice as its reference, noise suppression, gain control), input volume, Opus encode and decode, played back to the output device, with the level every 50 ms as `State::mic_test()` (`MicTest { level_db }`, a 1.5 dB-per-frame decay, floor -100 dB). It runs until stopped; it never joins or sends anything. With `AudioIo::Manual` it reads `MicCheck::feed_input` and writes `take_output` (`Session::mic_check()`), so tests and `--mock` never touch a real device. In a call it opens nothing new and reads the call's own level (`VoiceConnection::input_level_db`). Joining, leaving or moving ends it, and so do closing settings or leaving the page.

## Video

Receiving other people's cameras (v1 103), ported from oxidcord's `video/{rtp,receive,decode,mf}.rs` and `dave/codec.rs`. Facts from docs.discord.food voice connections ("Video", "Receiving Video", "Simulcasting", "Video RTP", read 2026-10-28).

- Op 12 (Video) from the server names a user, their `audio_ssrc`, primary `video_ssrc` and `streams` (`ssrc`, `rtx_ssrc`, `rid`, `quality`, `active`). Every active stream SSRC and the audio SSRC map to the user (the DAVE sender for decryption), the user joins the DAVE group's known users, and the first announcement raises `VoiceEvent::VideoStarted { user_id }`. Op 12 with `video_ssrc: 0` and no active stream, op 13, or a fresh connection (4006/4009) end it with `VoiceEvent::VideoStopped`. Your own op 12 echo is ignored. Packets for an SSRC no op 12 announced are dropped, as the docs ask. The session turns the events into `LocalEvent::RemoteVideo` (`VoiceSession::remote_video`), which makes that person's call tile a camera tile.
- Receive (`video/receive.rs`): payload type 101 packets go through the H.264 depacketizer (single NAL units, STAP-A, FU-A; a sequence gap, a broken fragment or a frame that never ends drops the frame and waits for a keyframe), then DAVE (`Codec::H264`: the NAL start codes and slice headers stay clear as unencrypted ranges, the rest is AES-GCM; `crates/dave/src/codec.rs`), then Media Foundation on the `video-decode` thread (one decoder per SSRC, at most 8 frames queued). RTX (102) is not repaired yet.
- Keyframe requests: a lost packet, a frame DAVE cannot open or a decode error sends an RTCP PLI (payload-specific feedback 206, format 1, from your audio SSRC to the stream's SSRC) through the transport, which keeps the 8-byte RTCP header as authenticated data, as the rtpsize modes do; at most one per SSRC a second, repeated each second until a keyframe arrives. A clean stream that starts on a keyframe sends none.
- Wants (op 15): `Command::SetVideoViews(Vec<VideoView { user_id, focused, width, height }>)` names the camera tiles on screen in device pixels. Every announced primary `video_ssrc` gets a quality: 100 for a focused tile, 50 for a thumbnail, 0 for anyone not on screen (the call view closed, scrolled away, or another tile focused); `pixelCounts` carries width times height for the tiles on screen, and `any` stays 100 as the handshake sent it. The connection resends wants whenever the views or the announced streams change and only when the payload differs (`VoiceConnection::set_video_wants`). Example: `{"9102": 100, "9202": 50, "9302": 0, "any": 100, "pixelCounts": {"9102": 921600, "9202": 57600}}`.
- `VoiceConnection::set_video_sink` takes a `video::VideoSink` (frames per stream, `ended(user)`); the session's is `session::VideoFeed` (`Session::video_feed`), which converts frames for the tiles on screen; turning them into pixels on screen and the measured cost are in `docs/media.md`, "Video". `video_users()` and `video_stats()` (received, decoded, dropped, keyframe requests) are for tests and diagnostics.
- Media Foundation must accept COM already set up on the thread (cpal's): the decode thread initialises COM as multithreaded and only undoes what it did itself.

## Go Live

Watching someone's stream (v1 106), ported from oxidcord's `session/src/task/streams.rs` (`crates/session/src/task/streams.rs`). Facts from docs.discord.food voice connections ("Streams") and gateway events ("Watch Stream", "Streams"), read 2026-10-28.

- A stream key is `guild:<guild>:<channel>:<owner>` (or `call:<channel>:<owner>`). Someone is live when their voice state has `self_stream: true`; the sidebar row shows LIVE and the call view adds a stream tile (`docs/ui.md`). Watching only ever starts from a click.
- `Command::WatchStream { stream_key }` needs you in that channel's call (otherwise "Join the voice channel to watch this stream."), stops any stream you were watching, sends op 20 and marks `VoiceSession::watching` at once. STREAM_CREATE gives `rtc_server_id` and `rtc_channel_id`, STREAM_SERVER_UPDATE the token and endpoint; with both, a second `VoiceConnection` connects with `server_id` = `rtc_server_id`, `channel_id` = `rtc_channel_id`, the main gateway session id, and `ConnectionKind::Stream`: identify offers one layer `{ type: "screen", rid: "100", quality: 100 }` and the DAVE group is `rtc_server_id - 1` (`VoiceParams::dave_group`). No answer within 15 seconds ends the view ("Couldn't connect to the stream.").
- The stream connection never sends media: it is always muted, and its audio is `AudioIo::Manual`, so it opens no device. The streamer's audio (op 12 on the stream connection maps the stream's `audio_ssrc` to the owner) is mixed by that connection every 20 ms and handed to the call's connection (`VoiceConnection::play_through` with the call's `playback_feed()`, at most 5 frames queued, the oldest dropped), which adds it to its own mixed frame after the call's master volume and before the echo canceller's reference and the device (v1 118; v1 113 played it from a second output stream, which kept it out of the echo canceller). Tests read it in the call's `take_output`, with the stream's SSRC in the frame's sources, and `VoiceStats::stream_frames_played` counts the frames the call played. The stream's own mix is scaled by the streamer's stream volume (`Command::SetStreamVolume { user_id, volume }`, 0 to 200 percent, `UserAudio::stream_volume` and `stream_gain`, applied with `VoiceConnection::set_user_volume` for the owner) and by the output volume setting. It is separate from the person's voice volume on the call connection, and is saved with it in kv `voice.user_audio` (`stream_volume` beside `volume` and `muted`). Deafen (yours or the server's) deafens the stream connection too; Stop Watching closes it and its audio with it. Its video goes to its own `session::VideoFeed` (`Session::stream_feed`), keyed by the owner, and its wants come from `Command::SetStreamViews` the way camera wants do. `Session::stream_connection()` hands it out for tests. The call's own connection, its DAVE group and its camera feed are untouched.
- A STREAM_SERVER_UPDATE with a new endpoint reconnects the stream connection; one without an endpoint drops it until a new server is announced. A stream connection that stops (`VoiceEvent::Stopped`) ends the view ("The stream connection was lost.").
- `Command::StopWatchingStream { stream_key }` sends op 19 and closes only the stream connection. Leaving voice, being moved out, or joining another channel ends every view with op 19; a fresh gateway session drops it without one.
- STREAM_DELETE for the stream you watch ends the view with a toast by `reason`: `stream_full` "This stream is full.", `user_requested` and `stream_ended` "The stream has ended.", `unauthorized` "You don't have permission to watch this stream.", `safety_guild_rate_limited` "Streams in this server are rate limited. Try again later.", anything else "The stream is no longer available."
- Going live yourself is "Going live" below; the capture it sends is "Screen capture", picked in the Go Live picker (`docs/ui.md`, call view).

## Going live

Streaming your screen or a window (v1 107), ported from oxidcord's `streams.rs` share role (`crates/session/src/task/go_live.rs`). Facts from docs.discord.food gateway events ("Create Stream", "Set Stream Paused", "Delete Stream") and voice connections ("Streams", "Video"), read 2026-10-28.

- `Command::GoLive { source: ScreenTarget, quality: VideoQuality, sound: bool }` needs you in a call (otherwise "Join a voice channel to go live.") and sends op 18 with the keys in the docs' order: `type` (`guild`, or `call` without a server), `guild_id`, `channel_id`, `preferred_region` (the first of READY's `geo_ordered_rtc_regions`, as op 4). The same choice again is ignored.
- Changing source while live (Discord's "Change Windows"; `Command::GoLive` with another source, quality or sound while a stream runs) swaps the capture behind the same stream connection, key and DAVE group, with no op 19 and no second op 18: `VoiceConnection::replace_screen` stops the old capture and starts the new one on the same layer, op 12 announces the new `max_resolution`, and the new encoder starts on a keyframe. The sound restarts for the new source. A swap made before the stream connection is up only changes what it will open.
- STREAM_CREATE for a key that ends in your user id gives the stream key, `rtc_server_id` and `rtc_channel_id`; STREAM_SERVER_UPDATE the token and endpoint. With both, a `ConnectionKind::Stream` connection identifies with the one `screen` layer, joins the DAVE group `rtc_server_id - 1`, and runs muted and deafened with `AudioIo::Manual` (no device is opened; the shared sound has its own source, below, "Stream sound"). No answer within 15 seconds ends it ("Couldn't start your stream."). `Session::share_connection()` hands it out for tests.
- Once connected, the capture opens (`video::open_screen` at the picked quality; `session::CameraIo::Pattern`, the test pattern at the source's fitted size, for tests and `--mock`) and `VoiceConnection::start_screen` runs the camera pipeline ("Camera" below) on Ready's one layer: op 12 with that layer as `type` `screen`, `max_resolution` the picked size, then DAVE-encrypted H.264 with playout-delay and RID 100. Viewers' wants arrive as op 15 on the stream connection and pause or resume the layer exactly as for the camera. The desktop picks the bitrate from the sent size and rate (2.5 Mbps at 1280x720 and 30 fps, scaled by pixels per second, 1 to 8 Mbps).
- `Command::SetStreamPaused(bool)` sends op 22 (`stream_key`, `paused`) and stops encoding and sending video and sound while paused (`VoiceConnection::set_video_paused`); resuming starts on a keyframe. A pause asked for before STREAM_CREATE is sent once the key is known. Minimising the shared window pauses the stream and restoring it resumes, as in Discord: the video pipeline reads `VideoSource::hidden()` (a window target that `IsIconic`) on every frame and raises `VoiceEvent::VideoSourceHidden(bool)` when it changes, and the session turns it into the pause. Discord answers op 22 with STREAM_UPDATE `paused`, which viewers (and your own tile) show as "Stream paused" ("Your stream is paused" on yours).
- Your own stream shows as a stream tile in the call view from the local frames, with no network: the stream connection's `VideoSink` is the session's stream feed (`Session::stream_feed`), so each captured frame reaches the tile under your user id before encoding, as your camera does on the call. Its views come with the watched streams' (`Command::SetStreamViews`); it can go full window; stopping the stream clears it. The video pipeline posts a preview frame only while it holds the sink and has not been stopped, so no frame reaches the tile after `stop_camera` or a swap.
- `Command::StopGoLive` stops the pipeline and the sound, closes only the stream connection and sends op 19; the call, its DAVE group and its camera are untouched. Leaving voice, being moved out or joining another channel stops it with op 19; a fresh gateway session drops it without one. STREAM_DELETE for your stream ends it with the reason's toast. The shared window closing (`VideoError::Ended`, `VoiceEvent::VideoSourceEnded`, raised at once rather than after 20 failed frames) ends it with "The window you picked was closed.", and a lost stream connection with "Your stream was disconnected."
- `VoiceSession::screen_share` is your stream while STREAM_CREATE has announced it; Discord also sets your voice state's `self_stream`, which is what other people see.

## Stream sound

Sharing sound with a stream (v1 118), as Discord does: a screen shares the whole computer's sound except kyoko's own (so people in the call don't hear their own voices come back), an app shares only that app's sound. The picker has Discord's Sound switch, on by default (`Command::GoLive { sound }`).

- Capture is WASAPI process loopback (Windows 10 2004 and later), `voice::open_loopback(SoundTarget)` in `crates/voice/src/sound/loopback.rs`: `ActivateAudioInterfaceAsync` on `VAD\Process_Loopback` with `AUDIOCLIENT_ACTIVATION_TYPE_PROCESS_LOOPBACK`. `SoundTarget::Everything { except }` uses `PROCESS_LOOPBACK_MODE_EXCLUDE_TARGET_PROCESS_TREE` on kyoko's own process id (a screen); `SoundTarget::App { process }` uses `PROCESS_LOOPBACK_MODE_INCLUDE_TARGET_PROCESS_TREE` on the shared window's process (`ScreenTarget::process`, from `GetWindowThreadProcessId`). Sharing kyoko's own window, or a window whose process is unknown, shares no sound. The client is opened shared, event driven, 48 kHz 16-bit stereo with `AUTOCONVERTPCM`, a 200 ms buffer, on an MTA thread; silent buffers become zeros.
- `VoiceConnection::start_sound(Box<dyn SoundSource>)` reads the source on a `stream-sound` thread into a ring of at most 2 seconds; `stop_sound` ends it, and closing the connection does too. 20 failed reads in a row stop it.
- The media loop takes 20 ms stereo frames from the ring and encodes them with a second Opus encoder (stereo, `Application::Audio`, 128 kbps, in-band FEC), DAVE-encrypts them as Opus and sends them from the stream connection's audio SSRC, which op 12 announces as `audio_ssrc`. The microphone chain (echo cancellation, noise suppression, gain control, input volume, gate) never touches it. The first frame sends op 5 with `speaking: 2` (soundshare: context audio for video, no speaking ring); a pause sends five Opus silence frames and op 5 with `speaking: 0`, and the next frame announces it again. `VoiceStats::sound_frames_sent` counts the frames.
- A sound source that fails to open leaves the stream going without sound (a warning in the log, no toast).
- Tests and `--mock` never capture real sound: with `session::CameraIo::Pattern` the session asks `VoiceSetup::capture` (`session::FakeCapture`) instead, which records each `SoundTarget` it was asked for and plays a 440 Hz tone at 0.25 (`voice::ToneSource`). The same seam's screen source reads `FakeCapture::set_minimised` as its `hidden()`. The real capture runs only by hand, on kyoko's own sound: `cargo run -p kyoko-voice --example own_sound` plays a 2-second 440 Hz tone at -29 dBFS from its own process and captures `SoundTarget::App` on its own process id (on this machine, 2026-10-29: 95,040 stereo frames at -28.2 dBFS). `PROPVARIANT` clears itself on drop (`PropVariantClear`, which frees a `VT_BLOB`'s data), so the activation blob that points at the stack is held in `ManuallyDrop`.

## Camera

Turning your own camera on (v1 104), ported from oxidcord's `video/{send,capture,encode}` (MIT/Apache; Media Foundation only, no openh264). Facts from docs.discord.food voice connections ("Video", "Sending Video", "Simulcasting", "Video RTP", "Native RTP Header Extensions", read 2026-10-28).

- Layers: identify offers `video` layers with `rid` 100 and 50; Ready's `streams` assigns each an `ssrc` and `rtx_ssrc` (`rtx_ssrc` defaults to `ssrc + 1`; a stream without an ssrc or rid is skipped). `VoiceConnection::video_layers()` lists them, highest quality first; a fresh connection (4006/4009) replaces them.
- `Command::SetSelfVideo(true)` (in a call only) opens the camera (`voice::video::open_camera` with `VoiceSettings.camera_device`, the first camera Windows lists when none is chosen; `session::CameraIo::Pattern`, the moving colour bars of `voice::video::TestPattern`, for tests and `--mock`) at `VideoQuality::preset(VoiceSettings.video_preset)` (0: 1280x720 at 30 fps and 2.5 Mbps, 1: 854x480, 2: 640x360), then `VoiceConnection::start_camera` builds one Media Foundation H.264 encoder per layer on the `video-send` thread. Only when both encoders open does the session send op 4 with `self_video: true` and the connection op 12.
- Op 12 keys, in the docs' order: `audio_ssrc`, `video_ssrc` and `rtx_ssrc` of the rid 100 layer, then `streams`, one per layer: `type` `video`, `rid`, `ssrc`, `active`, `quality`, `rtx_ssrc`, `max_bitrate`, `max_framerate`, `max_resolution` (`fixed`, the layer's size). Layer 100 is the preset; layer 50 is half the width and height at a quarter of the bitrate. Op 12 goes out before any video packet and again after a resume or a fresh connection.
- Sending: each captured NV12 frame is scaled to each layer (nearest sampling), encoded (constrained baseline, low-delay VBR, a keyframe every 2 seconds, SPS and PPS before every keyframe), DAVE-encrypted as `Codec::H264` (start codes and slice headers stay clear), split into payload type 101 packets of at most 1200 bytes (single NAL units, FU-A), and sent from the layer's SSRC with a 90 kHz timestamp shared by both layers and the marker on a frame's last packet. Every packet carries the one-byte (`0xBEDE`) extensions playout-delay (id 6, zero delay) and RID (id 11, the layer's rid); with the rtpsize modes they are encrypted with the payload. At most 8 encoded frames wait for the media task; older ones are dropped.
- Wants from the server (op 15): the value for a layer's SSRC, or `any`, sets that layer: 0 pauses it (nothing is encoded or sent for it, and op 12 marks it `active: false`), above 0 resumes it with a keyframe and op 12 marks it active again.
- An RTCP PLI or FIR from the server for a layer's SSRC asks that layer's encoder for a keyframe. RTX (102) and NACK are not answered yet, and no sender reports are sent.
- `Command::SetSelfVideo(false)` stops the pipeline, sends op 12 with `video_ssrc: 0`, `rtx_ssrc: 0` and no streams, and op 4 with `self_video: false`. Leaving, moving channels or a lost connection turns the camera off with the call. 20 failed frames in a row (an unplugged camera) stop it with `VoiceEvent::CameraStopped` and a toast.
- Your own frames also go to the call's `VideoSink` under your user id before encoding, so your tile previews them (`docs/media.md`, "Video").
- Without a Media Foundation H.264 encoder (Windows N without the Media Feature Pack), or with `session::VoiceSetup::codecs` set to `VideoCodecs::Missing` in tests, the camera never starts: no op 4 change and no op 12, a "Video unavailable" toast, and audio carries on. The desktop's `voice::video::available` probe now needs both an H.264 decoder and an encoder.
- Hardware encoders are not used: the NVIDIA and Intel H.264 MFTs on this machine are asynchronous and produced no output without a D3D11 device manager, so only synchronous (software) MFTs are enumerated. The Microsoft encoder needs `CODECAPI_AVLowLatencyMode` set before the media types, or it holds 16 frames back.

## Screen capture

Capturing a screen or a window for Go Live (v1 105), ported from oxidcord's `video/capture/win32/screen.rs` into `crates/video/src/screen.rs`. Going live sends it ("Going live" above).

- `voice::video::screens()` lists the monitors (primary first, named "Screen 1", "Screen 2", ... at their physical resolution) and the windows a person would share: visible, not minimised, not cloaked, not a tool window, not owned by another window, with a title, and not the shell's desktop (`Progman`) or taskbar (`Shell_TrayWnd`). Each is a `ScreenTarget { kind: ScreenKind::Screen | Window, id, name, width, height }`; `id` is the `HMONITOR` or `HWND`.
- `open_screen(target, VideoQuality)` starts a Windows.Graphics.Capture session (a free-threaded `Direct3D11CaptureFramePool` of two BGRA buffers on a hardware D3D11 device, WARP when there is none, the cursor captured, the yellow border turned off where Windows allows it) and is a `VideoSource` like the camera: each frame is copied to a staging texture, scaled (nearest sampling) and converted to NV12 (`video::bgra_to_nv12`, BT.601 limited range) to fit within the quality's width and height without upscaling, at the quality's fps. A screen that does not change sends no new frames, so the last frame repeats. The pool follows the window's size.
- Closing the window ends the capture: the capture item's `Closed` event, or `IsWindow` failing for a window target, makes `next_frame` return `VideoError::Ended`; `CameraPreview::start_until` stops on it at once and calls its end callback. 20 failed frames in a row end it too.
- `screen_thumbnail(target, width, height)` takes one frame without the cursor for the picker.
- `ScreenTarget::process` is the window's process id (0 for a screen), used for the window's sound ("Stream sound" above). `VideoSource::hidden()` is true while a shared window is minimised; Windows.Graphics.Capture then delivers no new frames, so the last one repeats until the stream pauses.
- Under `--mock` and in tests nothing real is captured: the desktop lists four fixed sources (Screen 1, Kyoko, Mock Browser, Mock Notes; the two mock windows carry made-up process ids) and previews them with `TestPattern`. `cargo run -p kyoko-video --example share_window -- <pid> <png>` captures one window of the given process, saves its first frame and reports when the window closes; it is the only way the real path runs, by hand, on your own window.

## Mockcord

Mockcord's voice server runs on TLS when mockcord is built with `tls` (its own self-signed root, `Server::voice().root_pem()`), plain `ws://` otherwise. It records each socket's ClientHello, ALPN and raw upgrade request, every frame both ways (with `seq` on the frames it numbers), closes, and every RTP packet it decrypts. `Server::voice()` also offers `set_modes`, `set_heartbeat_interval`, `close_sockets(code)`, `drop_sockets()` (no close frame) and `speak_as(user, ssrc, speaking)` for a scripted member's op 5. The UDP side answers IP discovery and echoes each packet back from SSRC + 1 in the mode the session selected.

Mockcord's voice server keys a socket's session by `server_id` and session id, so a call and a stream from the same gateway session live side by side; DAVE rooms, their mail and scripted broadcasts can be scoped to one channel.

On the gateway side, op 4 answers with VOICE_STATE_UPDATE (broadcast) and, only when the session's channel changes, VOICE_SERVER_UPDATE with the token `mock-voice-token-<channel id>` (the voice server accepts `mock-voice-token` and any token made from it this way). `Control::fill_voice_channel(id)` makes op 4 for that channel go unanswered, as a full channel. READY carries `geo_ordered_rtc_regions` `mock-east`, `mock-central`, `mock-west`. `VoiceControl::hold_echo()` keeps echoed packets back until `release_echo()`, so a test can send while hearing and let the echo arrive later (while deafened, say).

DAVE on mockcord: `VoiceControl::set_dave_version(1)` makes the session description announce DAVE v1 (the default is 0, so the echo tests and `--mock` stay plain). Each channel then has a DAVE room built from `dave::testing` (`Harness`): a `Gateway` that acts as the external sender (op 25, add and remove proposals, announcing the first matching commit, welcomes, transitions), the connected clients as external members whose DAVE frames go out over their sockets with the session `seq`, and scripted members that run kyoko's own `DaveSession` in process. `add_member(channel, user)` and `remove_member` add and remove a scripted member, `send_as(channel, user, ssrc, sequence, opus)` sends that member's DAVE-encrypted Opus over UDP to every client in the channel (pair it with `speak_as` so the client maps the SSRC), `downgrade_dave` and `upgrade_dave` send op 21 with version 0 and op 24 for epoch 1, and `send_bogus_commit(channel, user)` sends an op 29 that cannot be processed. Every RTP packet a client sends is also offered to the room's members, and `VoicePacket::opened` holds the frame one of them decrypted. Binary frames are recorded too (`VoiceFrame::is_binary`, `payload`).

Video on mockcord: `video_as(user, audio_ssrc, video_ssrc)` broadcasts a scripted member's op 12 with one active layer (`rid` 100, `rtx_ssrc` = `video_ssrc + 1`), `video_off_as(user, audio_ssrc)` the op 12 that clears it, `sink_wants(value)` sends an op 15 to every client, `request_keyframe(ssrc)` sends a PLI for a client's layer, and `connect_as(user)` / `disconnect_as(user)` a plain op 11 / op 13 (the DAVE room's own op 13 needs another scripted member to propose the removal). `ScriptedCamera::new(control, channel, user, ssrc)` plays the H.264 fixture `tools/mockcord/fixtures/video/camera.h264` (640x360, 15 fps, 30 frames, an IDR with SPS and PPS every 15; made once with `ffmpeg -f lavfi -i testsrc2=size=640x360:rate=15 -t 2 -pix_fmt nv12 -c:v h264_mf -hw_encoding 0 -rate_control quality -quality 40 -g 15 -bf 0 -f h264`, which encodes with Windows' Media Foundation H.264 encoder): `send_frame` seals the next access unit with that member's DAVE keys (plain when DAVE is off) and sends it as payload type 101 packets of at most 1200 bytes (FU-A), `lose_next_packet` drops one packet, and `play` sends at the clip's rate. `with_clip` plays any Annex B clip (`clip_frames` splits it at access unit delimiters). Identify's offered `streams` get SSRCs in Ready (after the audio SSRC and its echo, two per layer: `ssrc`, `rtx_ssrc`); a client's own video packets are not echoed but reassembled per SSRC and recorded once a frame's last packet arrives (`VoiceRecording::video`, `video_from(ssrc)`, `VoiceVideoFrame` with the sealed frame, the frame a room member opened with DAVE, its extensions, `rid()` and `is_keyframe()`), and a client's op 12 is relayed to the others with its `user_id`. RTCP packets from clients are opened and recorded (`VoiceRecording::rtcp`, `VoiceRtcp::is_picture_loss`, `media_ssrc`, `picture_losses(ssrc)`). `mockcord --cameras <tiles>:<fps>[:<clip>]` (`MockConfig::cameras`) makes the first `tiles` members of a channel (the seated ones first, then other members through op 11) turn on cameras when a client selects its protocol there; the `voice-video` capture uses `--cameras 1:15`.

Your own stream on mockcord: op 18 opens a `MockStream` for you (`VoiceControl::own_stream`, the next `rtc_server_id`, `rtc_channel_id` one above, DAVE group `rtc_server_id - 1`, nothing played into it), answers STREAM_CREATE and STREAM_SERVER_UPDATE (token `mock-voice-token-stream-<rtc_server_id>`) and broadcasts your voice state with `self_stream: true` (op 4 keeps it while you stream); op 19 for it removes it and broadcasts `self_stream: false`. Tests add a scripted viewer with `add_member(rtc_channel_id, user)` so your frames are opened. Go Live on mockcord: `Control::start_stream(guild, channel, owner)` makes a scripted streamer (VOICE_STATE_UPDATE with `self_stream: true`, a `MockStream` with `rtc_server_id` from 1278201813102755892 in steps of 4 and `rtc_channel_id` one above, a DAVE room for that channel in group `rtc_server_id - 1` with the owner as a member when DAVE is on, and the camera fixture playing into it as the owner's H.264). Op 20 for a scripted stream answers STREAM_CREATE and STREAM_SERVER_UPDATE (token `mock-voice-token-stream-<rtc_server_id>`, the voice server's endpoint); a client that selects its protocol in the stream channel gets the owner's op 11 and op 12 there only. `Control::fill_stream(key)` makes op 20 answer STREAM_DELETE `stream_full`; `Control::end_stream(key, reason)` stops it, broadcasts STREAM_DELETE with the reason and the owner's voice state without `self_stream`. Op 19 answers STREAM_DELETE `user_requested`. `mockcord --streams <n>` makes the first `n` seated members of every voice channel live from the start; the `voice-stream` captures use `--streams 1`.
